The process

01 · Discover
Brief to concept

Most clients say "make it bold." I move straight to mockups and iterate on their reactions instead of mood boards. Shown: the Pluto Plants McLaren 720S concept.

02 · Panel
Pre-press planning

Break the design into installable panels that follow the body lines, with overlaps and breakpoints planned for the installer. Pluto Plants alone was 21 print files and 41 GB across two cars. Shown: its two side print strips.

03 · Print
Roll-to-roll output

Colour calibration, scale test prints and file setup on the solvent printer. Mistakes caught here are the cheap ones. Shown: printed panels going on the Pluto Plants McLaren.

04 · Finish
On the road

Working beside installers taught me to design files that make their job easier: clean alignment, smart seams. Shown: the finished Pluto Plants McLaren.

Inside the factory floor

I manufactured structural carbon fibre components for low-volume, high-performance vehicles, from Oct 2025 to Jul 2026.

I took this role on purpose. After years of building software for people who make physical things, I wanted to work inside the environments that software is supposed to serve.

Side project · built in parallel with Multimatic

The carbon fibre sword

A Cyberpunk-inspired prop sword, made from scratch in carbon fibre with a programmable LED blade inside. Every step was new: making a mould, laying up composites, wiring the electronics and writing the code.

What it taught me

Mould making, composites work and AI-assisted programming. And that the fastest way to understand a manufacturing process is to fail at it in your own garage first.

~40 hours over 9 months - a three-layer carbon wet layup in low-volume composite materials, and 144 programmed LEDs.

Built at home, on my own time, with my own materials. No Multimatic tooling, processes, parts or IP.

01 · V1

A fast Halloween build: MDF shell, plexiglass, working LEDs. Rough, but it proved the idea.

02 · Mould

V1 became the plug. I sacrificed it to make the mould for the carbon version. Candle paraffin outperformed the filleting wax.

03 · Layup

Three layers of 2x2 twill, wet laid by hand into the mould halves using low-volume composite materials.

04 · Demould

Two carbon halves out of the mould, then trimmed, joined and clear-coated to a high gloss.

05 · Code

An Arduino Nano driving 144 addressable LEDs, programmed with AI assistance, built feature by feature.

06 · Lit

Fire, sparkle and glow modes on one button: tap to cycle, double-tap for power, hold to charge, release for a surge.

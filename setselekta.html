WHERE IT STARTED

It started as a clickable screenshot.

Ultra's 2024 set times in Figma - tap a set and it lit up. Screenshot your picks, send them to the crew.

Next came an add-your-own-sets idea. I dropped it fast.

Then iteration after iteration. The one thing I kept the whole way: the festival grid.

Early Figma prototypes, before the live app. Original set times: Ultra Music Festival.

The product

Problem

Festival apps plan for one person. Groups screenshot lineups and argue in chat, and someone always ends up alone at a set nobody else wanted.

The idea

Everyone votes on the sets they care about. SetSelekta turns the votes into one plan per day: when two sets clash, the one more of the crew wants wins.

In the field

Used by my own crew to plan EDC Orlando and Ultra Miami.

How it's built

Stack

Vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript on a PHP backend, built with AI-assisted development. Deliberately simple so one person can own spec, design, code, deploys and production bugs.

Under the hood

Gap detection for festival days that run past midnight, schedules exported as phone wallpapers rendered on canvas, picks synced across browser tabs, and layouts tuned across five breakpoints.

What I learned

AI made shipping a working app almost trivial. Getting it good was the hard part - the festival grid took the most rounds, because what made sense in my head wasn't usable on a phone screen. The site cost less than I expected. Merch, marketing and social are where it got expensive - every one costs either time or money, usually both.

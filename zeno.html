Zeno: making 3D wrap design production-ready

Zeno lets shops and brands customize a vehicle in real-time 3D, then export the files to print it. I joined xix3D as employee #9 and the first designer of any kind, and grew into owning the product.

xix3D
UX Designer → Product Manager

01

Print & Cut automation

Problem

Zeno could show a wrap beautifully in 3D, but what it exported couldn't go to a printer or plotter. Shops rebuilt every job by hand, so the design never left the screen.

My role

I owned the product definition for print and cut - PRDs, edge cases and acceptance testing - working with our integration developer and a contractor on the PDF and vector pipeline. I tested the exports in real production software at a wrap shop, and the first file out became a real wrap job.

Outcome

Three shop-ready export types: print files, cut files, and combined print-and-cut files. Zeno went from visualizer to the start of the production line.

Kiosk mode: 3M at SEMA 2024

Problem

3M wanted booth visitors at SEMA to explore its wrap films on real vehicles, live, and walk away with their build, on a fixed show deadline.

The call

Instead of building from scratch, the four 3M kiosks were an upgraded version of kiosks we'd already worked on. Reusing a proven build is what made the date realistic.

Outcome

Four kiosks at the centre of 3M's booth, which grew into a dedicated version of Zeno for 3M customers worldwide.

Ford Wraps Program: designing the service

Problem

Ford wanted to sell OEM-backed wraps directly to customers and route each order to a certified local installer. The screen was the easy part. The service behind it didn't exist.

My role

Alongside Ford's product designers, I designed the visualizer for every Ford vehicle in the program. I also owned what sat behind it: the back-end paperwork, the service blueprint, and the automations and integrations connecting customer, Ford, Zeno and installer.

Operations

Contacted ~700 wrap shops across the lower 48 and recruited ~350 into Ford's certified installer network, with the automations that handled shop comms at scale.

Outcome

The program didn't launch. It left a complete service model, a ~350-shop installer network and working comms automation, and taught me to design the system, not just the interface.

Visualizer prototype, Bronco

What I'd do differently

Ford's engineers were still working out how wraps affected self-driving sensors, and that was the real risk to launch. We spent that time perfecting email automations for edge cases. I'd have put that effort into the certified wrap shop network as an xix3D asset instead of a Ford one - a program built for one client dies with that client's priorities.
